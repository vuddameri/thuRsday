Hail, floods threaten Texas’ fall forecast as severe storm odds spike.  The next few months could be dicey, y'all.
While it may not feel like it now, Texas could be in store for a major cooldown in the coming months. But with “Super El Niño” in full swing, the National Weather Service warns that those chillier days and cold fronts could come with hail, flooding and severe storms. During a fall outlook update, Mack Morris, a meteorologist with the National Weather Service Austin-San Antonio office, painted a harrowing picture for the coming months. Apparently, a strong El Niño can mean a fall season with double the average rainfall in Texas. Though it’s surprisingly difficult to track a trend, as there have only been nine strong El Niño events since the 1950s, according to Morris.
“Typically, El Niño is more active in terms of overall severe reports,” Morris said. “Not usually as much in terms of tornadoes, but hail and wind are higher in terms of the reports compared to La Niña.”Fall-season hail reports nearly triple when comparing El Niño and La Niña years. However, these strong warm Pacific waters typically bring only a couple more hail reports than a neutral year.  Severe weather reports are likely to spike this fall. If the stars align, Texas could see a fall and winter season that’s as much as 6 degrees cooler than average. But what does all this rain mean for the persisting Texas drought?Some regions of the state, such as the Texas Hill Country and Edwards Plateau, have experienced flooding rains that lifted them out of persistent drought conditions. The Canyon Lake reservoir is “completely filled up” after the heavy dousing over the summer, says Morris.  But huge chunks of the state are still struggling with clear skies and sunny days. An active fall and winter season – lingering until spring of 2027 – may finally spell an end to the parched earth in the Lone Star State. But that’s yet to be solidified.
